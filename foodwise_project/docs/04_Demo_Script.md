# FOODWISE — presenter run sheet

## Six-minute talk track

### 1. Opening (20 seconds)

“Good morning. I’m [name], and this is FOODWISE: a public-data prototype that helps make food-price signals and household budget scenarios easier to see.”

### 2. Why this matters (55 seconds)

“The briefing figures in this concept put the number unable to afford a healthy diet at 43.5 to 43.9 million, and the daily cost at KSh 189 to 582 depending on the method. Those exact FAO series and reference years still need verification before external publication. Separately, KNBS reported 9.5 percent year-on-year inflation in Food and Non-Alcoholic Beverages in September 2026. That is a national CPI measure, not a market price.”

### 3. What the public data can show (55 seconds)

“FOODWISE uses the public WFP Kenya market-price file. It contains observations from January 2006 through September 2026, with different foods and markets reported at different times. The charts show monthly medians after explicit package sizes are converted to a per-kilogram or per-litre basis. The current coverage is not representative of every county. I will show a market actually present in the data, rather than imply we have Nairobi or Nyeri coverage.”

### 4. Three product modules (55 seconds)

“First is Price Intelligence: filter prices by food, place and date. Second is the Household Pressure Scenario: change the food quantities, household size and income, and see what the observed prices imply for that scenario. Third is Diet Gap Analysis. Food prices do not contain nutrient information, so that module waits for an expert-reviewed composition file and population-specific references rather than inventing micronutrient estimates.”

### 5. Live demo (75 seconds)

1. In **Price intelligence**, point out the food, market, unit, latest observation date and monthly trend.
2. In **Household simulator**, select a market with current observations. Keep the default household size and income or change them for the audience.
3. Change one quantity or the household income and show the result update.
4. Point out the coverage status. State if any item price is missing and explain that the shown total then understates the selected scenario.
5. In **Diet gap readiness**, show the template and clarify that this dataset is not yet supplied.

Suggested wording:

“This is the monthly cost of our selected scenario using the foods priced in this market. The quantities are editable demo assumptions, not a nutrition prescription. This share is a proposed signal, not a validated poverty threshold.”

### 6. Pilot opportunity (60 seconds)

“The next step is validation, not a bigger dashboard. We need one county partner to confirm useful markets and household groups, a nutrition expert to approve an affordable healthy-diet basket and nutrient references, and local partners to test price collection. The pilot can then check coverage, cost calculations and usefulness with the households and county teams who would rely on the output.”

### 7. Close (20 seconds)

“Healthy food should not be a luxury. FOODWISE makes the barriers visible so we can act on them. I’m looking for a county pilot partner, nutrition mentors and people who can help validate the data. Thank you.”

## Do not claim

- Do not say the WFP data represent all Kenyan households, all counties or Nairobi/Nyeri unless separate data establish that.
- Do not say a 60% income share causes diet diversity to collapse; this prototype does not measure household diet diversity.
- Do not call the editable scenario an FAO healthy-diet cost.
- Do not infer nutrient affordability from price alone.
- Do not state which KNBS food items increased the most without checking the September 2026 item-level CPI table.

## Q&A preparation

**Where does the data come from?** WFP Kenya food-price and market files on HDX; KNBS September 2026 CPI report for national inflation context.

**Why not show Nairobi?** The included recent WFP file does not give the same current, multi-food market coverage for Nairobi as for several other listed markets. Showing the actual source market is more honest than substituting another county.

**Is the basket nutritionally complete?** Not yet. The quantities are editable scenarios. A nutrition specialist and verified composition / reference data are required to make a diet adequacy claim.

**What will a pilot deliver?** A locally validated market panel, a reviewed healthy-diet basket, a transparent household affordability method, and partner feedback on whether the dashboard supports practical decisions.
